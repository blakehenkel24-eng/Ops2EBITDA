# Ops2EBITDA Competitor-Proven Content Strategy

**Date:** June 21, 2026  
**Research base:** `competitor-profiles/_summary.md`

## Strategic Choice

Ops2EBITDA should not compete on publishing volume or broad PE news. It should build the best public library for completing private equity operating work.

The editorial promise:

> Understand the issue. Diagnose the drivers. Produce the work product. Connect it to EBITDA, cash, and exit value.

Every priority piece must be searchable, useful without purchase, and connected to a concrete artifact.

## What “Proven by Competitors” Means

| Evidence tier | Standard | Examples in this research |
|---|---|---|
| A — strongest | Used by multiple competitors and tied directly to a commercial offer | Templates, workflow pages, product-led tutorials, case studies |
| B — strong | Supported by proprietary data or durable content architecture | Accordion surveys; Wall Street Prep knowledge hubs |
| C — directional | Visible on one or two current competitors but without public performance data | Annual AI use-case refreshes; nascent value-creation OS category pages |

The roadmap prioritizes Tier A and B patterns. Tier C items are tests, not assumptions.

## Five Content Pillars

### 1. Value Creation Planning and Execution

**Why it wins:** Maestro, ValoremIQ, and Accordion all organize their value proposition around planning, executing, measuring, and proving value creation. Search and forum evidence also show repeated demand for 100-day plans and VCP templates.

**Ops2EBITDA angle:** go deeper than project tracking. Show how to turn a thesis into initiatives, owners, KPI movement, an EBITDA bridge, and a board cadence.

**Commercial bridge:** Value Creation Plan Builder, Diligence-to-Execution Bundle, 30-60-100 Day Planner.

### 2. Operating Diagnostics and KPI-to-EBITDA Logic

**Why it wins:** Enterprise platforms sell visibility; Wall Street Prep succeeds with exact analytical concepts and worked models. The gap is interpreting operating KPIs in a PE context.

**Ops2EBITDA angle:** each page answers what moved, why it moved, what to analyze next, and how the result affects EBITDA/cash/value.

**Commercial bridge:** KPI Diagnostic Project, driver-tree and variance skills, Excel models.

### 3. Functional Value-Creation Models

**Why it wins:** Flevy validates paid demand for detailed downloadable toolkits; Wall Street Prep validates free-template-to-paid-product conversion.

**Ops2EBITDA angle:** narrower and more credible than a marketplace. One editorial standard, PE-specific assumptions, worked examples, and board-ready outputs.

**Commercial bridge:** pricing, procurement, working capital, sales productivity, retention, and synergy models.

### 4. Diligence-to-Execution Workflows

**Why it wins:** Blueflame publishes around diligence workflows; Maestro and Accordion connect diligence or early ownership to execution. Few competitors fully own the handoff.

**Ops2EBITDA angle:** what must survive close, how findings become initiatives, and how risks/assumptions are monitored after ownership begins.

**Commercial bridge:** Diligence & Investment Thesis Project, question builder, thesis stress tester, risk register.

### 5. AI Workflows for PE Operators

**Why it wins:** Blueflame demonstrates demand for named AI workflows, while enterprise complexity leaves a self-serve gap.

**Ops2EBITDA angle:** no generic prompt lists. Each piece includes required files, workflow steps, output schema, QA checks, and failure modes.

**Commercial bridge:** AI Project Kits, Skill Packages, Atlas IQ.

## Content Formats to Standardize

### The Operator Guide

Use for core search hubs. Structure:

1. Bottom line up front
2. When the issue matters in the hold period
3. Required data
4. Diagnostic logic
5. Worked example
6. KPI / EBITDA / cash impact
7. Common failure modes
8. Downloadable checklist or lite tool
9. Related paid asset

**Competitor validation:** Accordion’s BLUF/playbook format plus Wall Street Prep’s worked tutorials.

### The Free Work Product

A useful, limited artifact: checklist, scorecard, calculator, sample tab, question bank, or memo outline.

**Competitor validation:** Flevy’s previews/free packs and Wall Street Prep’s downloadable models.

### The Benchmark

Original survey or anonymized dataset with a flagship report, data pages, role cuts, industry cuts, and quarterly commentary.

**Competitor validation:** Accordion’s recurring sponsor/CFO research.

### The Workflow Page

Input → steps → output → QA → example → product.

**Competitor validation:** Blueflame’s use-case library and Maestro’s lifecycle framing.

### The Proof Page

Before/after artifact, worked case, or customer story with the actual output and measurable result.

**Competitor validation:** Maestro and Blueflame customer evidence; Flevy product inventories.

## The Flagship Research Franchise

### State of PE Value Creation Work

Survey operating partners, PE ops professionals, consultants, independent sponsors, and portfolio CFOs on:

- where VCPs live and how often they are updated;
- how initiatives are linked to KPIs and EBITDA;
- where diligence findings are lost after close;
- time spent producing board and portfolio-review materials;
- which analyses are rebuilt repeatedly;
- AI adoption by workflow;
- confidence in junior work product;
- gaps in functional operating capability.

The output should become:

- one flagship report;
- one interactive benchmark page;
- five role-specific articles;
- six workflow articles;
- a webinar or expert roundtable;
- a downloadable scorecard;
- 20–30 data-led LinkedIn posts;
- sales proof on relevant product pages.

Do not manufacture statistics. If sample size is small, publish the methodology and label the findings exploratory.

## Internal Linking and Conversion Architecture

```text
Definition / KPI page
        ↓
Operator guide or workflow page
        ↓
Free checklist / scorecard / lite model
        ↓
Single paid asset
        ↓
Relevant bundle
        ↓
Atlas IQ or related research/workflow content
```

Rules:

- Each commercial page links back to the guide that teaches the job.
- Each guide links to one primary asset, not a wall of undifferentiated products.
- Each free artifact captures an email only when the standalone value justifies it.
- Comparison pages link to proof pages and sample outputs, not merely feature claims.
- Industry playbooks link into functional clusters rather than becoming isolated silos.

## Product Page Standard

Flevy proves that specificity sells; Ops2EBITDA should make it more credible. Every product page needs:

- exact job and buyer;
- required inputs;
- included files/tabs/skills;
- screenshots or redacted previews;
- worked example;
- output list;
- QA and error checks;
- what the asset does not do;
- update date/version;
- related free guide;
- single-product and bundle CTA.

## Distribution System

One operator guide should yield:

- the full search article;
- a downloadable artifact;
- a five-post LinkedIn sequence;
- one carousel showing the workflow;
- one email with a worked example;
- one short Atlas IQ prompt/workflow;
- two internal links from existing knowledge pages;
- one product-page proof module.

This keeps distribution attached to the same research and avoids creating disconnected “social content.”

- expert review pass rate;
- citations/evidence freshness;
- percentage of pages with worked examples;
- percentage of paid products with previews and QA notes;
- update cadence for volatile AI and competitor content.

## What Not to Do

- Do not chase broad “what is private equity” traffic as the core strategy.
- Do not publish generic AI prompt listicles.
- Do not create dozens of comparison pages before product proof is visible.
- Do not imitate enterprise “single source of truth” language when the product is self-serve assets.
- Do not gate thin checklists.
- Do not claim competitor pricing, traffic, or weaknesses that cannot be verified.
- Do not let Atlas IQ obscure the broader product: models, project kits, skills, and operating knowledge are the moat.
