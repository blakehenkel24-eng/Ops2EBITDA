# Ops2EBITDA Tactical Content Execution Playbook

Date: May 10, 2026

## Purpose

This is the practical operating guide for executing the Ops2EBITDA content strategy. It covers the tools to use, channels to market through, media standards, article workflow, publishing process, weekly cadence, and AI prompts.

The goal is not to create a high-volume AI content farm. The goal is to publish serious, useful, buyer-stage content that moves a reader from:

```text
operating question -> diagnostic logic -> KPI / EBITDA implication -> work product -> relevant paid asset
```

## Operating Rule

Every article must have a job.

Before writing, answer:

1. Who is this for?
2. What work question are they trying to answer?
3. What artifact would help them do the work?
4. What paid Ops2EBITDA offer does this naturally point toward?
5. What visual, table, checklist, or example makes the article useful?

If you cannot answer those five questions, do not write the article yet.

### Do Not Buy Yet

Avoid these until you have evidence:

- Expensive marketing automation suites.
- Full programmatic SEO tools.
- Heavy webinar platforms.
- Paid social management tools beyond scheduling.
- Big design systems.
- Complex AI agent chains.

The first bottleneck is not tooling. It is choosing strong topics, publishing consistently, and proving that readers click into lead magnets or offers.

## AI Tool Roles

### ChatGPT / Codex

Use for:

- Turning source files into article briefs.
- Drafting article outlines.
- Editing for clarity and specificity.
- Creating tables, checklists, and examples.
- Writing lead magnet drafts.
- Generating LinkedIn/email/outreach variants.
- Updating repo files and internal links.

Best use:

- Give it the source files and the offer CTA.
- Ask for a structured brief before asking for a draft.
- Use a QA pass before publishing.

Avoid:

- Asking for a full article from a vague title.
- Letting it invent claims, benchmarks, or customer examples.

### Perplexity / Deep Research Tool

Use for:

- Finding market sources.
- Finding public examples.
- Checking competitor content.
- Finding current reports and data points.
- Building source lists for serious articles.

Best use:

- Use it before writing articles that mention market trends, industry data, or competitor gaps.
- Save source links in the article brief.

Avoid:

- Copying language from sources.
- Publishing source-backed claims without opening and checking the original.

### Ahrefs / Semrush

Use for:

- Keyword validation.
- Competitor content review.
- Search intent checks.
- Finding related questions.
- Finding internal-link opportunities later.

Best use:

- Validate the top 20-30 topics, not every small idea.
- Use keyword data to choose titles and headings, not to distort the content.

Avoid:

- Chasing high-volume beginner keywords with weak buyer fit.

### Canva / Figma

Use for:

- Lead magnet PDFs.
- Simple diagnostic frameworks.
- LinkedIn carousel graphics.
- Article diagrams.
- Model preview screenshots.
- Checklist one-pagers.

Best use:

- Keep visuals clean, practical, and consulting-grade.
- Make diagrams that clarify the work, not decorative graphics.

Avoid:

- Stock photos of handshakes, skyscrapers, generic finance imagery, or vague AI imagery.

### Buffer

Use for:

- Scheduling LinkedIn posts.
- Reposting article snippets.
- Tracking post performance.
- Managing cross-posts to X, Threads, and possibly Bluesky.

Best use:

- Schedule the week's distribution immediately after publishing an article.

Avoid:

- Posting the same generic teaser across every channel.

## Channels To Market Through

### Channel 1: SEO / Owned Website

Role:

- Capture existing demand.
- Build long-term authority.
- Make the product discoverable through search and AI answers.

Best topics:

- Private equity 100-day plan.
- Operational diligence checklist.
- KPI driver tree examples.
- Working capital improvement private equity.
- Pricing waterfall analysis.
- Search fund first 100 days.

Execution:

1. Publish 3-4 serious articles per month.
2. Each article links to 2-4 existing knowledge base pages.
3. Each article has one lead magnet or paid offer CTA.
4. Add FAQ sections where helpful.
5. Refresh articles monthly based on GSC data.

Success metrics:

- Search impressions.
- Clicks.
- Time on page.
- Lead magnet conversions.
- Paid offer clicks.

### Channel 2: LinkedIn

Role:

- Build authority with PE consultants, operators, independent sponsors, and boutique advisors.
- Test which ideas resonate before turning them into bigger assets.

Best formats:

- Short POV posts.
- Diagnostic checklists.
- Failure-mode posts.
- Carousel summaries.
- Before/after work product examples.
- "Question I would ask before close" posts.

Cadence:

- 3-5 posts per week.
- 1 longer post tied to each article.
- 2-3 shorter snippets from the article.
- 1 practical checklist or visual.

Post types:

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Contrarian POV | Most value creation plans are diligence artifacts, not operating blueprints. |
| Checklist | 7 questions to ask before calling a KPI problem an execution problem. |
| Failure mode | Why 100-day plans drift by day 45. |
| Artifact preview | A simple diligence-to-execution handoff map. |
| Persona-specific | What search fund CEOs should diagnose before changing anything. |

Success metrics:

- Comments from target buyers.
- Saves.
- Profile visits.
- DM replies.
- Clicks to article or lead magnet.

### Channel 3: Direct Outreach

Role:

- Get the first qualified conversations and buyers faster than waiting for SEO.

Targets:

- PE value creation consultants.
- Independent sponsors.
- Search fund CEOs.
- Boutique diligence/advisory firms.
- PE ops associates.

Execution:

1. Build a list of 50-100 target people.
2. Send useful assets, not generic sales pitches.
3. Ask for feedback before asking for a purchase.
4. Track replies and objections.

Example note:

```text
I put together a practical checklist on the questions that separate real PE value creation from slideware.

It is built around diligence -> KPI -> EBITDA -> execution handoff, so it is less "thought leadership" and more work-product oriented.

Would it be useful if I sent it over?
```

Success metrics:

- Reply rate.
- Asset requests.
- Feedback calls.
- Checkout intent.
- Objection patterns.

### Channel 4: Searchfunder And Search Communities

Role:

- Reach independent sponsors, searchers, and acquisition entrepreneurs with acute operating needs.

Best topics:

- First 100 days after acquisition.
- Diagnosing before changing the business.
- Working capital and cash conversion.
- Customer concentration.
- Management meeting cadence.
- Board/investor updates.

Execution:

- Participate with useful answers.
- Share checklists only when relevant.
- Avoid drive-by promotion.
- Translate PE jargon into operator language.

Success metrics:

- Replies.
- Profile clicks.
- Asset requests.
- Conversations with searchers.

### Channel 5: Reddit / Wall Street Oasis

Role:

- Listen for language and pain.
- Occasionally answer high-fit questions.

Best use:

- Research, not heavy promotion.
- Extract FAQs and objections.
- Identify content gaps.

Execution:

- Search weekly for questions about PE ops, value creation, diligence, search fund 100-day plans, KPI dashboards, operating partner work.
- Save language into the topic backlog.
- Answer only when you can be genuinely useful.

Success metrics:

- New topic ideas.
- Language fragments.
- Questions worth turning into articles.

Cadence:

Format:

## How To Write An Article

### Step 1: Choose The Topic

Use this filter:

```text
Is this a real work question for a target buyer?
Does it connect to an existing Ops2EBITDA source asset?
Does it connect to a lead magnet or paid offer?
Can we include a useful table, checklist, or diagram?
```

Example:

```text
Topic: Operational Diligence Questions by Function
Audience: diligence consultant / PE associate
Work question: What should I ask management before close?
Source assets: operational diligence content, diligence skills, management interview skills
CTA: Diligence Research Skills or Diligence & Investment Thesis Project
Visual: function-by-function question matrix
```

### Step 2: Research The Intent

Do three checks:

1. Google the target query and read the top results.
2. Search forums for how people ask the question.
3. Check whether existing content is too generic, too theoretical, or too software-led.

Capture:

- Common headings.
- Missing practical details.
- Language used by buyers.
- Source links.
- Content gap Ops2EBITDA can fill.

### Step 3: Create The Brief

Use the brief template.

The brief should decide the article before anyone drafts it.

Required output:

- Title.
- Audience.
- Search intent.
- Outline.
- Internal links.
- Visual.
- Lead magnet.
- Paid CTA.
- Claims to verify.

### Step 4: Draft The Article

Use this structure:

```text
H1: Clear title

Intro:
- Name the work problem.
- State why it matters in PE.
- Preview the practical artifact.

Section 1:
- Explain the operating context.

Section 2:
- Show the diagnostic logic.

Section 3:
- Connect to KPIs / EBITDA / cash / valuation.

Section 4:
- Include table, checklist, matrix, or driver tree.

Section 5:
- Show common mistakes or failure modes.

Section 6:
- Explain what work product to create next.

CTA:
- Lead magnet or paid asset.

FAQ:
- 3-5 search-driven questions.
```

### Step 5: Add The Practical Artifact

Every serious article needs one artifact.

Examples:

- 100-day plan checklist.
- Diligence question matrix.
- KPI driver tree.
- Risk register template.
- Data request list.
- Board update outline.
- Model preview.

This is what makes the article feel useful instead of generic.

### Step 6: Run The QA Pass

Ask AI to critique:

```text
Review this Ops2EBITDA article.

Check for:
- Generic PE education
- Weak operating specificity
- Missing KPI / EBITDA / cash / valuation linkage
- Unsupported claims
- Weak CTA
- Missing internal links
- Missing useful artifact
- AI-sounding language

Return a prioritized fix list.
```

Then edit manually.

### Step 7: Prepare The Publishing Bundle

For each article, create:

| Asset | Requirement |
|---|---|
| SEO title | Clear and search-aligned |
| Meta description | Practical promise in 150-160 characters |
| URL slug | Simple and readable |
| Summary | 2-3 sentence article summary |
| Social posts | 3 LinkedIn variants |
| Email teaser | 1 short newsletter blurb |
| Lead magnet CTA | One primary CTA |
| Paid offer CTA | One secondary CTA |
| Internal links | 2-4 knowledge base links |
| Visual | One article graphic or table |

### Step 8: Publish

Publishing checklist:

1. Add article to the website or repo content system.
2. Add title, meta description, and clean slug.
3. Add internal links.
4. Add lead magnet or offer CTA.
5. Add image alt text.
6. Preview on desktop and mobile.
7. Check links.
8. Submit URL to Google Search Console after publishing.
9. Add the article to the distribution calendar.

### Step 9: Distribute

For one article, publish:

- Day 1: LinkedIn POV post.
- Day 2: Email newsletter.
- Day 3: Checklist or diagram post.
- Day 5: Failure-mode post.
- Day 7: Direct outreach using the article or lead magnet.
- Day 14: Repost a different angle if the topic performed.

### Step 10: Measure

After 2 weeks, record:

- Page views.
- Search impressions.
- Social engagement.
- Email clicks.
- Lead magnet downloads.
- Offer clicks.
- Replies or conversations.

Then decide:

- Refresh article.
- Create a lead magnet.
- Build a paid offer page.
- Write a follow-up article.
- Drop the topic.

- 3-4 articles.

Only do this once the process is smooth:

- 2 articles per week.
- 5 LinkedIn posts per week.
- 1 email per week.
- 30-50 outreach touches per week.
- 1 new visual asset per article.

## Article Prompt Pack

### Brief Prompt

```text
Create an Ops2EBITDA content brief.

Topic: [topic]
Audience: [audience]
Buyer stage: [stage]
Target keyword family: [keywords]
Source assets: [source files]
Primary CTA: [lead magnet or paid offer]

The brief must include:
- Search intent or shareable thesis
- The work question the reader is trying to answer
- Recommended title
- Outline
- Required table/checklist/diagram
- Internal links
- Lead magnet CTA
- Paid offer CTA
- Claims that need source support
- What this article must avoid

Editorial standard:
Connect operating issue -> diagnostic question -> KPI -> EBITDA/cash/valuation implication -> work product.
```

### Draft Prompt

```text
Write a first draft from this brief.

Rules:
- Serious, practical, consulting-grade tone.
- No generic PE education.
- Make the article useful under time pressure.
- Include at least one table, checklist, matrix, or driver tree.
- Connect the topic to KPI, EBITDA, cash, risk, or valuation.
- End with one useful CTA.
- Do not invent customer quotes, benchmarks, or proprietary claims.
```

### LinkedIn Repurposing Prompt

```text
Turn this article into 3 LinkedIn posts:

1. Contrarian POV.
2. Practical checklist.
3. Failure mode or example.

Audience: [audience]
CTA: [article / lead magnet / offer]

Keep the posts specific, plainspoken, and operator-grade. Avoid hype.
```

### Lead Magnet Prompt

```text
Create a 1-3 page lead magnet outline for this article.

It should be useful as a standalone artifact and naturally connect to [paid offer].

Include:
- Title
- Sections
- Checklist/table/matrix
- Short intro
- CTA
- Suggested visual layout
```

## Current Tool Notes

Tool pricing and features change often. As of this guide date, current public pages indicate:

- ChatGPT has individual, business, and enterprise tiers, with Business offering a secure workspace and admin controls.
- Ahrefs has Lite, Standard, Advanced, and Enterprise plans, with keyword, content, site audit, and AI visibility features.
- Buffer supports planning, publishing, engagement, analytics, and multiple social channels, with pricing based on connected channels.
- Perplexity Max is positioned for high-volume research and advanced model access, but is expensive relative to early-stage needs.
- Canva Pro is useful for individual creators who need premium templates, brand kit, background removal, and AI design features.

For Ops2EBITDA right now, the best practical stack is:

```text
ChatGPT/Codex + Perplexity + Google Search Console + Canva + Buffer + Beehiiv/ConvertKit + Notion/Airtable
```

Upgrade SEO tooling only after the first 10-12 articles and 2-3 lead magnets are live.
