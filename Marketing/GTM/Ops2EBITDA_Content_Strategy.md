# Ops2EBITDA Content Strategy

Date: May 9, 2026

## Strategic Objective

Use content to make Ops2EBITDA the practical reference layer for PE value creation work: the place business-fluent professionals go when they need to understand an operating issue, connect it to KPIs and EBITDA, and produce sharper diligence, board, or execution work product.

The content strategy should not chase broad "private equity education" traffic. It should attract people close to the work:

- PE consultants and diligence consultants
- emerging PE ops and value creation professionals
- independent sponsors and search fund operators
- boutique PE advisory teams
- operating partners and value creation leaders as influencers

## Strategy Thesis

Ops2EBITDA already has a strong content base. The main opportunity is to turn that base into buyer-stage content paths:

1. **Searchable reference content** captures existing demand around PE value creation, 100-day planning, diligence, KPIs, working capital, pricing, sales productivity, churn, procurement, and operating cadence.
2. **Shareable point-of-view content** creates demand by naming the shift from slideware value creation to execution-ready operating work.
3. **Template and diagnostic content** converts attention into leads by giving users useful artifacts that naturally bridge into the paid catalog.

The best content should answer a work question the user is already under pressure to solve.

## Content Positioning

### Content Promise

> Practical PE operating content that helps you move from issue to action to KPI to EBITDA impact.

### Editorial Standard

Every major piece should connect at least three of these five layers:

1. Operating issue
2. Diagnostic question
3. KPI or metric
4. EBITDA / cash / valuation implication
5. Work product the user needs to produce

### Tone

- Serious
- Operator-grade
- Specific
- Consultant-sharp
- Plainspoken
- Useful under time pressure

### Avoid

- Beginner "what is private equity?" positioning as the main brand face
- Generic AI or chatbot content
- Vague "operational alpha" claims
- Thin listicles with no PE operating logic
- Founder newsletter vibes

## Searchable Content Architecture

For V1, most new editorial content can live under `/blog` or `/insights` with strong internal linking. Do not overbuild a complex hub structure before measuring demand.

Recommended URL families:

- `/insights/private-equity-100-day-plan`
- `/insights/operational-diligence-questions`
- `/insights/kpi-driver-tree-examples`
- `/insights/pricing-waterfall-analysis-private-equity`
- `/insights/working-capital-improvement-private-equity`
- `/insights/search-fund-first-100-days`

Recommended internal linking pattern:

- Every article links to 2-4 relevant existing knowledge base entries.
- Every article links to one relevant paid offer or lead magnet.
- Every paid offer page links back to 2-3 educational articles that explain the problem.
- KPI articles link laterally to related KPIs and vertically to playbooks.

## Competitive / Market Content Gaps

The broader market has lots of content about PE value creation, but much of it falls into predictable buckets:

- consulting-firm thought leadership that is credible but not very usable
- software content that frames everything around its platform
- SEO articles that talk about 100-day plans without enough functional specificity
- financial training content focused more on modeling and interview preparation than operating execution
- search fund advice that is practical but scattered across posts and stories

Ops2EBITDA can win by being:

1. More practical than consulting reports
2. More PE-specific than generic operating templates
3. More execution-oriented than finance training
4. More structured than forum advice
5. More artifact-driven than most thought leadership

## Editorial Rules

1. Every article should answer a real operating question.
2. Every article should include at least one table, checklist, driver tree, or example artifact.
3. Every article should link to a next action: related knowledge base entry, lead magnet, or paid asset.
4. Every article should preserve the free / paid distinction:
   - Free content teaches the logic.
   - Paid assets help the user do the work.
5. Do not publish generic PE explainers unless they connect directly to a buyer-stage path.
